# Deck Outline

> Six slides maximum. The live demo is the main event; slides frame it before and drive decisions after. Source everything from SCENARIO.md.

## Open (0–8 min)

### Slide 1: What I Heard

- Start with intros: each panelist gives their role and what they hope to get from today
- The problem, the stakes in $, and why now (SCENARIO §1)
- Assumptions to confirm: "I read this as X for finance and Y for platform. Did I frame that right?" (SCENARIO §8)

### Slide 2: The Answer

- What I built, the value in $, and my recommendation, in three lines. Then go straight to the demo.

## Demo (8–25 min)

### Slide 3: Live Demo (switch to the workspace)

- Walk the demo path (SCENARIO §7), telling the business story as you go
- Name the seat as you go: platform (how it works and scales), finance ($), head of data (fit and adoption)
- Briefly show how it was built and deployed (AI-assisted build, deployed with DABs). This is part of what's assessed and gives the platform lead proof it will operationalize.
- Pause after key moments and invite reactions
- Hard stop at ~25 min to protect time for positioning and questions
- Fallback: have screenshots ready outside the deck

## Position and Decide (25–45 min)

### Slide 4: Root Cause and Business Case

- Primary, secondary, and tertiary drivers with $ impact (SCENARIO §3)
- $ saved, risk removed, TCO (SCENARIO §6)

### Slide 5: My Recommendation

- One recommendation, why it wins, and what we give up (SCENARIO §5–6)
- Tradeoffs I made in the prototype and what a real engagement does next (SCENARIO §9)
- Future-proofing: AI and agentic use cases on the same platform

### Slide 6: Decisions We Need Today

- 2–3 concrete asks, with next steps, owners, and a timeline (SCENARIO §8)

## Debrief (45–60 min, no slides)

- Be honest about what you'd do differently, own the tradeoffs, and stay curious about their feedback.
