Here’s how it works so there are no surprises: you get a business-escalation scenario about 4 hours before your slot. You use that window to build a working prototype with AI-assisted tools. Then you have a 60-minute panel where you present and position what you built to three panelists playing senior customer stakeholders. The last 15 minutes, they drop the role play and debrief you directly. Let me walk you through how to be excellent at each piece.

WHAT THEY’RE ACTUALLY EVALUATING This is the most important thing to internalize: they are NOT just grading what you build. They’re grading how you think, how you communicate, and how well you connect the build back to a real business problem. A modest prototype that clearly solves the escalation and is positioned crisply to the right stakeholders will beat an impressive-but-unfocused build every time. Think, communicate, connect, in that order.

THE 4-HOUR BUILD WINDOW: SPEND THE FIRST 30 MINUTES NOT BUILDING The temptation will be to start coding immediately. Don’t. Your discovery happens HERE, against the scenario doc, this is where “do the discovery” really lives in this format, not as cold questions in the room. Before you touch a tool:

- Read the escalation twice. What is the REAL business problem underneath the ask? Escalations are rarely about what they literally say, find the pain and the stakes.
- Map the three personas to the scenario. What does each one care about? What would make each say “yes, this helps me”?
- Decide what success looks like and scope ruthlessly to it. A narrow thing that works end-to-end beats a broad thing that half-works. Pick the smallest prototype that convincingly proves the point.
- THEN build. Use the AI-assisted tools aggressively and confidently, leveraging them well is part of what’s being assessed. But you own the output; understand what you shipped well enough to defend every choice.
- Protect the last 30 to 45 minutes for the story, not the code. Rehearse the walk-through, prep your framing, and make sure the demo runs. A working demo you can narrate calmly is worth more than one more feature.

KNOW YOUR PERSONAS COLD, AND SPEAK TO EACH SEAT You have three specific stakeholders in the room. Position deliberately to each:

- Platform Engineering Lead: cares HOW it works, architecture, does it scale, is it secure, will it operationalize, will it break in production. Give them substance and show you understand the platform.
- Budget Owner / IT Finance: cares about cost, ROI, TCO, and “why now.” Frame value in dollars and risk reduced, not features. Have a point of view on cost.
- Head of Data / Platform Owner: cares about strategic fit, adoption, and whether this actually solves the business problem and moves the org forward. Connect your build to their bigger picture. Name who you’re speaking to as you go (“For the platform side, here’s how this scales, and for finance, here’s what that means for cost”). Reading the room and tailoring to the seat is exactly the skill being tested. Also, spend the first 5 minutes asking stakeholders to introduce themselves, and ask them to give you a quick summary of what they were hoping to accomplish in this session.

DISCOVERY IN THE ROOM = CONFIRM AND CLARIFY, NOT COLD-QUESTION Because you already have the scenario, don’t open with a blank-slate discovery interrogation, you’d look like you didn’t do the prep. Instead, show you did your homework by stating the assumptions you built on and inviting them to confirm or correct: “I read this as primarily a cost-control problem for finance with a scale concern for platform, did I frame that right?” A few sharp confirming questions to the personas shows judgment and adaptability without wasting the room’s time. Then adjust live based on what they tell you.

DRIVE THE ROOM AND GET TO THE DEMO QUICKLY It’s an escalation, there’s urgency, so act like it. Lead with the problem and the outcome, then show the working thing fast. Don’t spend ten minutes on preamble or architecture theory before they see it run.

- Open crisp: here’s the problem I heard, here’s what I built, here’s the value, then demo.
- Show it working early. A prototype that actually runs earns you instant credibility; narrate as you go.
- Then position: tie every part back to the personas’ concerns and the business outcome.
- Pauses are your friend.
- Keep an eye on time, protect room for positioning and for their questions. Don’t let the build walkthrough eat the whole hour. 

IT’S A PROTOTYPE: OWN THE TRADEOFFS Nobody expects production-grade in 4 hours. What impresses is you being clear-eyed about it: “I stubbed this piece to focus on proving X; in a real engagement I’d do Y next.” Narrating your tradeoffs and your “what’s next” shows senior judgment. Never bluff, if you didn’t get to something, say so and say how you’d approach it. That’s a strong answer, not a weak one. Also, remember, showing a demo is more powerful than a slide deck alone; leverage both. If you have the time, also be prepared to go through the root causes of the secondary and tertiary cost drivers. This will show thoughtfulness and remind the stakeholders that Databricks is a platform that can future-proof their needs, like future AI or Agentic use cases.

THE LAST 15 MINUTES: THE DIRECT DEBRIEF When they drop the role play, shift gears. This is where they see if you’re reflective and coachable. Be honest about what you’d do differently, own your tradeoffs, and engage with their feedback with curiosity. Coachability is a real signal here.

A FEW THINGS TO FOCUS ON, BASED ON HOW THE PROCESS HAS GONE SO FAR 

You’ve been strong and consistent all the way through, so these are sharpening notes, not fixes:

- Be prescriptive. A theme in your feedback is that you can lean more into a clear recommendation rather than laying out options and leaving the room to choose. In this round, land on a point of view and defend it: “Here’s what I recommend and why, here’s what I’d give up.” Driving the room to two or three concrete decisions is exactly what’s scored.
- Talk value in business and dollar terms, not just technical ones. You naturally frame things in cost and reliability; push that one step further into outcomes and dollars for the finance and Head-of-Data seats (what this saves, what risk it removes, why now). Connect every technical choice back to the business problem.
- Your technical depth is not in question here, so spend your energy leading the room. The coding and architecture rounds already answered that. This round is 75% customer skills, so put your effort into positioning, persona-tailoring, and driving to decisions rather than proving more depth.
PRACTICAL PREP

- Block the full 5-hour day (4-hr build + 1-hr interview) and guard it.
- Have your AI-assisted tooling set up, tested, and warmed up before the build window opens, don’t lose build time to setup.
- Test your screen share, audio, and demo path ahead of time. Have a fallback (screenshots) in case the live demo hiccups.
- Rehearse your opening 60 seconds cold, a smooth open sets your confidence for the whole hour.
